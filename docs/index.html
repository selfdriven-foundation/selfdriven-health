Why together

The real changes happen where they overlap.

Mental and physical

Poor sleep lowers mood, and low mood disrupts sleep. Exercise helps break the loop: in a 2024 review of 218 trials, walking or jogging, yoga and strength training all reduced symptoms of depression.

Mental and developmental

Stress narrows attention, which makes new things harder to learn. Settling your nervous system first is often the fastest way to think clearly again.

Developmental and physical

Balance, coordination and strength respond to practice at any age. Learning a new movement trains your body and your brain at the same time.

Health is how well you can feel, grow and move, all at the same time.

Five checks before you
trust a health post.

Supplement threads spread fast and often link to a shop. Here's a typical one, with the questions worth asking before you buy anything.

  1. Who said it, and where?

    "Someone famous takes it" reports a habit. It isn't evidence that it works for you. Find the original interview or study before you act on a summary of it.

  2. What does the poster earn?

    "The one I personally use" next to a shop link usually means a commission on each sale. That doesn't make the advice wrong, but it's a reason to check it yourself.

  3. What's on the back of the bottle?

    The big number on the front is the total oil or powder. The supplement facts panel shows the active amount, such as EPA + DHA in fish oil, which can be a fraction of the front label.

  4. How does the dose compare with the safe limit?

    The Australian upper level of intake for vitamin D in adults is 80 µg (3,200 IU) a day. 10,000 IU is about three times that. Higher doses are sometimes prescribed after a blood test, but they aren't a safe default.

  5. Was it tested on people like you?

    Age, pregnancy, health conditions and other medicines all change what's safe. Ask your pharmacist about interactions, and your GP whether a blood test makes sense first.

How this site works

Four commitments.

Graded by evidence

Habits in the body guides are marked solid, moderate, limited or unproven, with the studies listed at the end.

Nothing to sell you

No affiliate links, discount codes or sponsored products. When a guide names a dose, it compares it with Australian limits.

Your data stays with you

Check-ins, checklists and timers are saved in your own browser. Nothing you enter here is sent to us.

Alongside your GP

The guides help you understand your body and prepare better questions. They don't diagnose or replace care from a professional.
