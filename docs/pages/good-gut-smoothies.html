Six smoothies that keep the fibre in.

A blender keeps the whole fruit and vegetable in the cup, where a juicer throws the pulp away. These six share one simple formula. Here is what each can do for your gut, what none of them can, and a week planner with a shopping list.

01 · Why blend

A blender keeps what a juicer throws away.

Most of a fruit’s fibre sits in its skin, pulp and seeds. A juicer strains those out. A personal blender keeps the whole fruit or vegetable, and that fibre is what your gut bacteria live on.

Where the fibre goes

  1. Step 1

    Into the cup

    Oats, seeds, skins and leaves, blended whole.

  2. Step 2

    Past the small intestine

    Fibre and resistant starch aren’t digested on the way through.[12]

  3. Step 3

    Fermented in the colon

    Gut bacteria break them down and make short-chain fatty acids, such as butyrate.

  4. Step 4

    Fuel for the lining

    Butyrate is the main fuel for the cells lining the colon.[13]

What a smoothie can do, in three numbers

+1.5a week
More complete bowel movements with 2 green kiwifruit a day

184 adults in New Zealand, Italy and Japan, 4 weeks per treatment. Funded by Zespri, and the fruit was eaten whole.[1]

19of 93
Inflammatory proteins that fell when people ate more fermented food

36 healthy adults over 10 weeks, IL-6 among them. Gut microbe diversity rose. In the high-fibre group it didn’t change.[3]

17%
Of Australian adults reach the fibre target that lowers disease risk

That target is 28 g a day for women and 38 g for men. Median intake was about 21 g in the 2011–12 survey, the latest with fibre in grams.[9][10]

What about gut repair?

No food or smoothie has been shown to repair the gut lining in people without a diagnosed gut disease, and leaky gut is a description rather than a diagnosis.[18][19] What these smoothies can do is smaller and real: add fibre toward your daily target, add live cultures, and, with kiwifruit, ease constipation.

02 · The smoothies

Six smoothies, one formula.

Each is a single serve for a personal blender cup. They share the same building blocks, so you can rotate them across a week.

Australian measures: 1 tbsp is 20 mL and 1 cup is 250 mL. Fill to your cup’s max line, and never blend hot liquid in a sealed personal-blender cup. Drink it fresh, or keep it covered in the fridge and finish it the same day.
01

Morning gut smoothie

Best for: Everyday, with breakfast

  • ½banana, firm and slightly green
  • ½ cupblueberries (frozen is fine)
  • 2 tbspplain Greek yoghurt or kefir
  • 1 tbspchia seeds
  • 1 tbsprolled oats
  • 200–250 mLwater or milk

Oats and chia add fibre, and the yoghurt or kefir adds live cultures. A firm banana carries far more resistant starch than a ripe one, and gut bacteria ferment that starch.[12]

Ripe or frozen-ripe banana tastes sweeter but has little resistant starch left. Buy a few green and use them first.

≈1 fruit serveLive culturesLow FODMAP if lactose-free
02

Ginger–kiwi

Best for: Regular bowel movements

  • 2green kiwifruit, peeled
  • 1 small piecefresh ginger
  • ½cucumber
  • 1 handfulbaby spinach
  • ½lemon, juiced
  • 200 mLcold water
  • Ice

Two green kiwifruit a day is the best-tested food for constipation: about 1½ more complete bowel movements a week after 4 weeks.[1] The trial used whole fruit. Blending keeps the fibre, but blended kiwi hasn’t been tested.

Give it 4 weeks. Guidelines suggest 2–3 kiwifruit a day for at least that long before you judge it.[2]

≈1 fruit serveLow FODMAP
03

Apple–oat fibre drink

Best for: Three kinds of fibre in one cup

  • ½–1apple, skin on
  • 2 tbsprolled oats
  • 1 tbspground flaxseed
  • Pinchcinnamon
  • 200 mLmilk, kefir or water

Skin-on apple, oats and ground flax each bring a different fibre. Use ground flax; whole seeds tend to pass straight through. Let it sit 5–10 minutes after blending and it thickens.

Bloat easily? Apple is high FODMAP at any useful amount. Swap in 2 kiwifruit or ½ cup strawberries.[22]

½–1 fruit serveHigher FODMAP: apple
04

Green gut smoothie

Best for: The most vegetables

  • ½cucumber
  • 1 handfulbaby spinach
  • ½green apple
  • ½avocado
  • A few leavesfresh mint
  • Squeezelime
  • 200–250 mLwater

The avocado makes it creamy without dairy and adds fibre and unsaturated fat. It’s the lowest in fruit of the six.

On warfarin? A daily spinach smoothie is a big change in vitamin K. Keep greens steady and tell your doctor first.[28]

≈½ fruit serveHigher FODMAP: apple, avocado
05

Berry kefir

Best for: The simplest fibre-plus-cultures pair

  • 200 mLplain, unsweetened kefir
  • ½ cupmixed berries
  • 1 tbspchia seeds or ground flaxseed
  • Pinchcinnamon
  • Optional ½banana

Berries and chia bring the fibre; kefir brings live cultures. In a 10-week trial, eating more fermented food raised gut microbe diversity in healthy adults.[3]

Live cultures aren’t the same as probiotics. That word needs a named strain with trial evidence behind it.[25]

½–1 fruit serveLive culturesLactose: kefir
06

Pineapple–ginger

Best for: A cold, light one for hot days

  • ¾ cuppineapple
  • 1 small piecefresh ginger
  • ½cucumber
  • A few leavesfresh mint
  • 200 mLcoconut water or water
  • Ice

Very refreshing, and the only one of the six with no seeds or oats, so it’s the lowest in fibre. Add 1 tbsp chia to lift it. Keep the pineapple to about this amount.

Coconut water is high in potassium, and above about 100 mL it’s no longer low FODMAP. Half water, half coconut water works.[22][27]

≈¾ fruit serveLow FODMAP with water
03 · Build your own

One fruit, one vegetable, fibre, a fermented food, liquid.

That formula sits behind all six. Pick one of each and the card shows what you’ve made, how much fruit it counts as, and anything to watch.

What not to build

Banana, mango, dates, honey and juice looks healthy. It’s close to three days of fruit.

A banana, a cup of mango, two Medjool dates and a cup of juice comes to about 5½ serves of fruit against a guideline of 2 a day, plus a tablespoon of honey.[8] The UK’s NHS counts sugar in juice and blended fruit as free sugar, and suggests no more than 150 mL of juice and smoothies a day combined.[7]

Blending isn’t the problem. In small studies, whole fruit blended with its fibre and seeds raised blood sugar no more than eating it whole.[6] The amount is the problem. Keep to about one fruit serve and add chia, flax or oats. If you have diabetes, check how a smoothie affects your glucose.

04 · Your goal

Match the smoothie to what you’re after.

Ingredients that feed a diverse microbiome can make an irritated gut feel worse, so the best pick depends on the goal.

The one goal with good food evidence.

Two green kiwifruit a day for 4 weeks added about 1½ complete bowel movements a week in people with chronic constipation, and did better than psyllium across the two constipated groups.[1] The British Dietetic Association now recommends 2–3 kiwifruit a day for at least 4 weeks.[2]

What helps

  • The ginger–kiwi smoothie every day for 4 weeks before you judge it.
  • The other foods with guideline support: 8–10 prunes a day, rye bread, and high-mineral water. Psyllium is the supplement with the best evidence.[2]
  • More fluid as your fibre goes up.[11]

Watch

  • “Eat more fibre” on its own isn’t well supported for constipation. Name the food instead.[2]
  • The trial used whole kiwifruit. Blended kiwi keeps the fibre but hasn’t been tested.
  • See your GP about a change in bowel habit that lasts, especially with bleeding or weight loss.[31]
Picks: Ginger–kiwiApple–oat

Go slowly, and lean on low-FODMAP picks.

Fibre that feeds bacteria also makes gas, so adding a lot at once often makes wind worse. In the one diet trial in constipation, the lower-fibre group reported less wind and bloating.[2] Monash University rates most ingredients here as low FODMAP at these amounts.[22]

What helps

  • Ginger–kiwi, pineapple–ginger made with water, and the morning smoothie with a firm banana and lactose-free yoghurt.
  • Halve the chia, flax and oats for the first week, then build up.
  • Keep to about one fruit serve per smoothie. Small FODMAP amounts in one sitting add up.[21]

Watch

  • Apple is high FODMAP at any useful amount, avocado is high at a standard serve, and ripe banana is high above about a third of one.[22][24]
  • Kefir and yoghurt carry lactose. That only matters if you’re lactose intolerant, and lactose-free versions exist.[23]
  • The low FODMAP diet is a short, three-step plan for diagnosed IBS, best done with a dietitian. Have ongoing bloating checked by a GP first.[20]
Picks: Ginger–kiwiPineapple–gingerMorning

Nothing has been shown to speed recovery.

No food or capsule has been shown to bring your gut back faster after antibiotics. In one small study, a multi-strain probiotic capsule slowed recovery for months.[4] Eating normally, with plenty of plant variety and some fermented food, is the reasonable default.

What helps

  • Yoghurt or kefir with live cultures on most days. In a 10-week trial in healthy adults, this raised gut microbe diversity.[3]
  • Variety over volume: rotate the six rather than repeating one.
  • Ease fibre back in if your gut is unsettled.

Watch

  • Diarrhoea that is severe, bloody, or goes on for days during or after antibiotics needs a GP.
  • Skip probiotic capsules unless your doctor suggests a specific one.[4]
Picks: Berry kefirMorningGreen

For most people, the win is reaching the fibre target.

Adults need 25 g of fibre a day (women) or 30 g (men), and 28 g or 38 g to lower chronic disease risk. Fewer than 1 in 5 Australian adults reach that.[9][10] A smoothie can add a useful few grams on top of your meals.

What helps

  • Rotate all six for different fibres: oats, chia, flax, fruit skins and leaves.
  • Fermented food on some days.[3]
  • Count each smoothie’s fruit toward your 2 serves a day.[8]

Watch

  • A smoothie adds to meals. It doesn’t replace the vegetables, legumes and wholegrains on your plate.
  • Fruit adds up fast. Keep to about one serve per cup.
Picks: all six, in rotation

06 · The evidence

What the evidence supports.

Graded from solid to not shown. Most gut-health claims rest on small trials or lab work, and the grades say which.

Solid
Blending keeps fibre that juicing removes.
Most fibre is in the skin and pulp. A medium orange has about 2.7 g of fibre; half a cup of its juice has about 0.2 g.[5]
Guideline
Adults need 25–30 g of fibre a day.
NHMRC: 25 g for women and 30 g for men, and 28 g and 38 g to lower chronic disease risk. Median adult intake was about 21 g.[9][10]
Moderate
Two green kiwifruit a day ease constipation.
184 adults, 4 weeks: about +1.5 complete bowel movements a week in functional constipation and IBS-C, better than psyllium overall. Industry-funded, and now in UK dietetic guidelines at 2–3 a day for 4 weeks or more.[1][2]
Moderate
Ginger eases nausea in pregnancy.
12 trials, 1,278 pregnant women: nausea improved, vomiting didn’t clearly change. Most trials used capsules of about 1–1.5 g a day, more than a slice in a smoothie.[15]
Limited
Fermented foods increase gut microbe diversity.
One 10-week trial in 36 healthy adults: diversity rose and 19 of 93 inflammatory proteins fell. Its main immune outcome didn’t change.[3]
Limited
Green bananas feed butyrate-making bacteria.
The least-ripe bananas held about 37% starch by dry weight against 3% when fully ripe, and most of it reached the large bowel undigested. Human trials of green banana for gut health are few.[12][13]
Limited
Ginger helps digestion and bloating.
In a small trial in indigestion, ginger sped stomach emptying without easing symptoms. The one IBS trial found no benefit.[15][16]
Limited
Oats in a smoothie lower cholesterol.
Trials used 3 g or more of oat beta-glucan a day, which lowered LDL by about 0.25 mmol/L. A spoon or two of oats gives far less.[14]
Limited
Berry polyphenols feed your good bacteria.
Mostly lab and animal work. A 2022 review found 3 human trials and no overall effect on gut bacteria.[17]
Not shown
Blended fruit spikes blood sugar more than whole fruit.
In three small studies, fruit blended with its fibre and seeds raised blood sugar the same as or less than the same fruit eaten whole. Sweetened shop smoothies weren’t tested.[6]
Not shown
A smoothie repairs a leaky gut.
Leaky gut is a description, not a diagnosis. A 2024 review found no strong evidence that diet changes gut permeability in healthy people.[18][19]
Not shown
Probiotics restore your gut after antibiotics.
In 21 adults, a multi-strain probiotic delayed the gut’s return to normal for months. One small study of one product.[4]
Myth
Live cultures mean probiotic.
In Australia, yoghurt and fermented milk must carry at least 1 million cultures per gram. A probiotic is a named strain with trial evidence of benefit.[25]
07 · Check first

Check these before a daily smoothie habit.

General guidance, not a plan for you. If any of these apply, the recipes may need changes, and a GP or Accredited Practising Dietitian can tailor them.

Build fibre slowly

Add chia, flax and oats gradually and drink more as you go, because fibre takes up water.[11] Never eat chia dry and wash it down. One man needed an endoscopy when dry chia swelled in his oesophagus. Blend or soak it.[30]

Kidney disease

Banana, avocado, kiwifruit, spinach and coconut water are high in potassium. Not everyone with kidney disease needs to limit it, so ask your renal team or dietitian before a daily smoothie habit.[27]

On warfarin

Spinach is high in vitamin K. Don’t avoid it, but keep the amount about the same each week. A new daily green smoothie is a big change, so tell your doctor first. You may need extra INR blood tests.[28]

Allergies

An itchy, tingly mouth from raw kiwi is usually oral allergy syndrome and mild, but kiwi can also cause a true allergy, rarely anaphylaxis. With a latex allergy, banana, avocado, kiwifruit and strawberry are common cross-reactors. Yoghurt and kefir contain milk.[26]

Pregnant

Kefir and yoghurt made from pasteurised milk are fine. Avoid raw-milk or homemade kefir of unknown milk, and ready-to-drink juices or smoothies sold unpasteurised. A smoothie you make at home with washed fruit is fine.[29]

A gut condition

With Crohn’s disease, a known narrowing of the bowel, or during an IBD flare, ask your gastroenterologist or dietitian before adding seeds and skins. With diagnosed IBS, the low FODMAP diet works best with a dietitian.[20] Find one at Dietitians Australia.

See a GP, whatever you’re drinking

No smoothie fixes these. Get them checked.

  • Blood in your poo, or black poo
  • A change in bowel habit that lasts: looser, narrower or more often
  • Weight loss you can’t explain
  • Ongoing tummy pain, cramping or bloating
  • Tiredness, or low iron found on a blood test

Bowel screening is free from 45

From 45 to 74 you can do the free home test every 2 years. At 45–49 you ask for your first kit at ncsr.gov.au or on 1800 627 701, or through your GP.[31]
