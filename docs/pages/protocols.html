The book · what it is

One reference to come back to, not a book to read once.

Huberman is a Stanford neuroscientist and host of the Huberman Lab podcast. Protocols is his first book. It turns years of the podcast's material into practices, and explains the biology behind each one so you can adapt it to your own life.[1]

7areas

Sleep, exercise, stress control, nutrition, light, focus and learning, and personal growth.[1]

~50protocols

Steven Bartlett's count when Huberman came on Diary of a CEO to launch the book.[4]

688pages

The Australian trade paperback. The US hardcover from Simon Element runs to 512.[2][3]

Kind one

Foundations

Daily behaviours that lift your baseline mood, energy and health, whatever else you are working on.

Kind two

Targeted tools

Specific protocols for a common problem or goal, like falling asleep, calming down fast or learning a skill.

How to read it. Front to back, or open the chapter you need. Some protocols rest on strong trials and others on early or mechanistic research. Because each one comes with its reasoning, you can tell which is which.

Seven areas · one way to start in each

Pick an area. Try one thing this week.

For each area, here is what the book covers and one place to start, drawn from what Huberman has published or said about it. The source sits under each one.

Sleep

Feel
Start with

Get outside into daylight as soon as you can after waking.

Morning light sets your body clock, so you get sleepy at the right time that night. The book's own publicity uses this as its example of a simple sleep protocol.[1]

Australian note: morning UV can climb fast in summer. Use sun protection whenever the UV index is 3 or above.[12]

Source: official book page and FAQ

Exercise

Move
Start with

Two resistance sessions this week, each under an hour of hard work.

The book covers getting stronger and leaner at any age.[1] His published Foundational Fitness Protocol mixes resistance, cardio and endurance work across the week, trains each muscle group twice (once directly, once indirectly) and caps weight sessions at about 50 to 60 minutes after a warm-up.[7]

Source: Huberman Lab fitness protocolFascia movement guide

Stress control

Feel
Start with

Two breaths in through the nose, then one long breath out through the mouth.

This is the physiological sigh. On Diary of a CEO he called it the most effective tool he knows for bringing stress down quickly.[5]

In a 2023 Stanford study he co-authored, five minutes a day of cyclic sighing for a month lifted mood more than mindfulness meditation did.[8]

Source: DOAC interview, Cell Reports MedicineVagus nerve breathing guide

Nutrition

Move
Start with

Take a short walk after your biggest meal of the day.

The book uses this as an example of a protocol that takes only minutes: walking after you eat helps your body manage blood glucose.[1]

Source: official book FAQAnti-inflammatory bowls

Light

Feel
Start with

Bright light early, dim warm light late.

His published light routine: 10 to 15 minutes of sunlight soon after waking, some time outside around midday, a little sun before sunset, then dim, warm indoor lighting at night.[1]

Overcast daylight outdoors still counts. Light through a window does not.[5] Pair it with sun protection when the UV index is 3 or above.[12]

Source: Huberman Lab, DOAC interview

Focus, motivation & learning

Grow
Start with

Practise just past the point where you start making mistakes.

He describes learning as driven by challenge and errors, done while you are alert enough to notice them.[5] The book adds protocols for learning faster.[1]

Source: DOAC interview, book description

Personal growth

Grow
Start with

Do one small hard thing a day that you would rather skip.

In the same interview he linked willpower to a brain region, the anterior mid-cingulate cortex, and treated it as something you can build.[6] The daily hard thing is our way in; the chapter goes further.

Source: DOAC interview summary
Behaviours first · the order the book works in

Foundations at the base. Supplements at the top, and smallest.

The book is built in layers. Supplements and medicines can add to a strong base, but in his account they do little without one.[6]

1

Foundations

Light, sleep, movement, food and breathing. Daily, mostly free, and the largest effect.

2

Targeted tools

A specific protocol for the problem in front of you.

3

Supplements

Adjuncts, added on top.

“Foundational behaviors come first, before supplements, technology, or products.”Huberman Lab, official book FAQ[1]

The thread · five supplements, checked

Saw the “five supplements” thread? Check these first.

On Diary of a CEO, Steven Bartlett asked Huberman which five supplements he would keep for life.[4] Summaries of his answer are circulating on X with shop links attached. Here is what to check before buying, against Australian limits.

What the thread says
Check in Australia
Magnesium
Bisglycinate, for sleep
Check the dose

The adult upper limit for magnesium from supplements is 350 mg a day. Diarrhoea is usually the first sign of too much.[10]

Omega-3
At least 1 g EPA a day
Read the label

The thread is right that 1,000 mg of fish oil is not 1,000 mg of EPA and DHA. Read the back of the bottle. If you take blood-thinning medication, ask your GP first.

Vitamin D
5,000–10,000 IU a day
Above the Australian limit

The adult upper level is 80 µg, or 3,200 IU a day. Both ends of that range sit above it. Ask your GP for a blood test and dose to the result.[9]

Alpha-GPC
600–900 mg
Ask your GP first

A Korean study of 12 million adults over 50 found users had about 43% higher stroke risk over ten years. It cannot prove cause, but it is a reason to ask first. The episode's own fact-check sheet also flags a caveat.[11][5]

Creatine
5–10 g a day
Well studied

One of the most researched supplements for both effect and safety. The usual maintenance dose is 3–5 g a day.[13]

The products linked in those threads are the posting account's choices, sold through Amazon. Huberman did not pick them, and neither did we. Supplements can interact with medicines, so check with your GP or pharmacist before starting one.
