Feel · Depression

Depression isn’t sadness.
It’s the dimmer switch.

It isn’t the opposite of happiness either. Depression turns down interest, energy, sleep, appetite, focus and self-worth, and often feeling itself. This guide covers what it is, why the low-serotonin story didn’t hold up, and why doing small things that matter before you feel like it, a therapy called behavioural activation, is one of the best-tested ways back.

01 · Not sadness

Sadness is a feeling. Depression is a state.

Sadness is usually about something, and it moves. Depression can arrive without a reason, outlast the reason it had, and flatten everything else on the way, good feelings and bad ones alike.

Grief can turn into depression, and the two can sit together. If the right-hand column has fit for two weeks or more, book a GP appointment.

It isn’t the opposite of happiness either.

Mood runs from sad to happy. Depression sits on a different axis: how alive things feel. That’s why someone can smile through a dinner and still be depressed, and why grief, painful as it is, usually isn’t.

“The opposite of depression is not happiness, but vitality.”
Andrew Solomon · TEDxMet, 2013

Pushing for happiness aims right on that map. Recovery mostly comes from moving up: interest, energy, contact and doing things that matter.

Chasing happy may not help. In a 2014 study of people who’d had depression, those who valued happiness most had more symptoms, and as a group they valued happiness more than people who’d never been depressed.

02 · The nine signs

Of nine signs doctors ask about, one is feeling down.

This is the PHQ-9, one of the most widely used depression questionnaires. Four of its nine questions are about your body. To be diagnosed you need low mood or lost interest, so depression is possible without feeling sad at all.

03 · The serotonin story

The low-serotonin story didn’t hold up.

For decades depression was explained as a chemical imbalance: too little serotonin, topped up by a pill. It was simple and it eased stigma. When researchers pulled the evidence together, it wasn’t there.

1950sAccident

Two drugs lift mood by chance

Iproniazid, a tuberculosis drug, and imipramine, tested for other conditions, both improve mood in some patients. Both act on brain chemicals called monoamines.

1965–67Hypothesis

Reasoning backwards

If drugs that raise these chemicals help, perhaps depression is a shortage of them. Noradrenaline is proposed first, then serotonin.

1987–90sMarketing

Prozac, and “chemical imbalance”

Fluoxetine reaches the US market and the SSRI era begins. A serotonin shortage becomes the standard public explanation, much of it through advertising.

2014Side effect

The story changes how people cope

People with current or past depression who were told (via a fake test) that a chemical imbalance caused it didn’t blame themselves less. They grew more pessimistic about recovery and less confident they could manage their own mood.

Kemp, Lickel & Deacon · Behaviour Research and Therapy
Jul 2022Review

No consistent evidence of a shortage

An umbrella review led by Joanna Moncrieff at UCL pooled decades of work on serotonin in blood and brain fluid, its receptors and transporter, artificial depletion, and genes. It found no convincing evidence that depression is caused by low serotonin.

Moncrieff et al. · Molecular Psychiatry
Dec 2022Counterpoint

Some evidence of reduced release

A brain-imaging study of 17 people with depression and 20 without found a reduced capacity to release serotonin in depression. It’s a small study, and the authors said serotonin problems are unlikely to explain everything about depression.

Erritzoe et al. · Biological Psychiatry
NowConsensus

Many causes, interacting

Genes, ongoing stress, loss, loneliness, trauma, poor sleep, inflammation, physical illness and some medications all contribute. Serotonin may play a part for some people. No single chemical explains depression.

What this does and doesn’t mean.

It’s still real, and still physical

Depression changes sleep, appetite, stress hormones and how the brain learns from experience. A one-chemical story was too simple. Plenty is still going on in the body and brain.

Antidepressants still help many people

All 21 antidepressants in the largest comparison beat placebo over about eight weeks. Average effects were modest, and some people respond strongly. Serotonin rises within hours of a dose but mood shifts over weeks, a clue that they work through slower changes.

522 trials116,477 adultsLancet 2018

Don’t stop suddenly

Coming off antidepressants can cause withdrawal effects. The 2022 review’s own authors advised talking to a health professional first. If you want to stop, plan a gradual reduction with your prescriber.

The story you believe changes what you do

An explanation with no levers makes recovery look further away. You have many: sleep, movement, people, therapy, medication and purpose all act on depression. You can pull more than one at once.

04 · Purpose

Motivation comes after you start.

Depression says to wait until you feel like it. Waiting makes the day smaller, and a smaller day gives you fewer reasons to feel like anything. Purpose here doesn’t mean a life mission. It means one small thing that matters to you, done before the feeling arrives.

The shrinking loop

Waiting to feel like it

  1. Feel flat. Nothing seems worth the effort.
  2. Wait to feel like it. Cancel, put off, stay in.
  3. Do less. Fewer people, less movement, more time to ruminate.
  4. Fewer good moments. Less contact, less achievement, less pleasure.
Round again, a little flatter
The activation loop

Acting on what matters

  1. Pick one small thing that matters. Small enough that starting is easy.
  2. Do it before you feel like it. Schedule it, then follow the plan, not the mood.
  3. Notice what it gave you. A little contact, achievement or pleasure. Even 1% counts.
  4. Slightly more energy. Enough to pick the next small thing.
Round again, a little wider
Behavioural activation · Lancet 2016

Scheduling what matters is a therapy

In the COBRA trial, 440 adults with depression got either behavioural activation from junior workers with five days’ training, or CBT from therapists. A year on, both groups had dropped from about 17 to about 8 on the PHQ-9, the same scale as section 02. Activation cost about a fifth less.

440 adultsPHQ-9 ~17 → ~8~21% cheaper
Exercise · BMJ 2024

Moving counts, and harder counts more

A review of 218 trials found walking or jogging, yoga and strength training each brought moderate drops in depression. Harder sessions helped more than light ones. Yoga and strength training had the fewest dropouts. The authors rated the certainty of this evidence low to very low, so treat the size of the effect with caution.

218 trials14,170 peoplewalk · yoga · strength
Ask what matters, not what you feel like.

“What would I do today if it mattered?” gets a different answer from “What do I feel like doing?” The first question is where purpose starts. The 4Cs give you four places to look.

06 · When to get help

See someone soon if any of these are true.

Small moves help, and they work best alongside proper care. Each of these is a reason to talk to a person, not a page.

You’re thinking about death, suicide or hurting yourself

Or that people would be better off without you. You don’t need to be sure it’s serious enough to call.

Lifeline 13 11 14Suicide Call Back Service 1300 659 467Immediate danger 000

Low mood with voices, or beliefs others find strange

Hearing or seeing things others don’t, or becoming convinced of things that worry the people around you. This needs an assessment today: call your GP or go to an emergency department.

It’s been two weeks or more and life is slipping

Work, study, home or relationships are suffering. Book a GP appointment this week. A GP also checks for physical causes that look like depression, such as an underactive thyroid, anaemia, sleep apnoea or a medication side effect.

You’ve also had highs

Stretches of unusual energy, little need for sleep, racing ideas or risky spending. Tell your GP before starting an antidepressant, because depression in bipolar disorder is treated differently.

You’re pregnant or have a new baby

Depression around pregnancy and birth is common and treatable, for mums and dads.

PANDA 1300 726 306

You’re drinking or using more to get through

Alcohol eases the evening and deepens the next day.

National Alcohol and Other Drug Hotline 1800 250 015

07 · Someone you love

If someone you love has gone flat.

You can’t lift it for them. You can make the next step easier and keep showing up.

What helps

  • Ask directly about suicide if you’re worried. Asking doesn’t put the idea in someone’s head. It tells them they’re allowed to say it.
  • Skip “cheer up”. It aims at happiness. Invite them to do one small thing with you instead: a walk, a coffee, an errand.
  • Make the first step smaller. Offer to book the GP, drive them there, or sit with them while they call.
  • Keep inviting. Expect “no” for a while. The invitation itself tells them they still belong.
  • Look after yourself too. Most services below support family and friends as well.

If you’re worried about their safety

Stay with them if you can, and call Lifeline together. If they’re in immediate danger, call 000. You can also call Lifeline about someone else, any time.
